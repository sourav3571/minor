# References and further reading

The lessons are self-contained; these sources provide original definitions and implementation details. Check the versioned documentation that matches your installed software.

1. PyTorch, [Learn the Basics](https://pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/basics/intro.html) and [torch.nn](https://pytorch.org/docs/stable/nn.html).
2. LeCun et al., “Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition,” *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 1998 (CNN history and LeNet).
3. Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean, “Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network,” 2015, arXiv:1503.02531 (output distillation and temperature).
4. Deb et al., “A Fast and Elitist Multiobjective Genetic Algorithm: NSGA-II,” *IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation*, 2002 (non-dominated sorting and diversity).
5. Zitzler, Laumanns, and Thiele, “SPEA2: Improving the Strength Pareto Evolutionary Algorithm,” 2001 (multi-objective evolutionary optimization).
6. Real et al., “Large-Scale Evolution of Image Classifiers,” *ICML*, 2017 (evolutionary architecture search).
7. Tan and Le, “EfficientNet: Rethinking Model Scaling for Convolutional Neural Networks,” *ICML*, 2019 (model scaling context).
8. PyTorch [reproducibility notes](https://pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/randomness.html) and [benchmarking notes](https://pytorch.org/tutorials/recipes/recipes/benchmark.html).
9. ONNX [documentation](https://onnx.ai/onnx/) and NVIDIA TensorRT [documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/tensorrt/).

A literature review should cite sources you personally read, use the official version/bibliographic details required by your college, and distinguish an author’s measured claim from your own result. A citation is not evidence that your implementation reproduces a paper.
